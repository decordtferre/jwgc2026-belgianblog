Today's task turned into one of the more talked-about days of the competition so far, after a wave system formed over the task area. For Wannes and Michiel, catching it became the story of their flights. For Edwige, missing it turned into a different kind of story altogether - one about resetting, having fun, and getting home anyway.

===

## [Michiel](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1192965)

Michiel's day began with a radio call from Wannes: he'd found wave, and was flying it with the French. Michiel went looking for it himself, and found it too. "Super cool," as he put it. He spent the first 50 kilometres of the task riding the wave, a little too slowly but thoroughly enjoyable, before switching over to racing under the cumulus clouds, where the lift was occasionally very strong. The only sting came right at the end: the last good climb took just long enough to arrive that a clean final glide was out of reach. Even so, it added up to another hugely instructive day and a consistent performance that moved him up two more places in the rankings.

## [Edwige](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1193476)

Edwige took a different approach entirely: forget the racing, just have fun. It worked. The sky looked unpromising before takeoff, but conditions turned out far better than expected, except Edwige couldn't get into the wave before the start, and ended up starting almost 1,000 metres lower than the pilots who did.

She flew most of the first leg alone before linking up with the Hungarian team, and later the South Africans. After missing a climb with the group past the second turnpoint, she found herself very low, and from there the only goal left was getting home. The final glide wasn't an easy one to catch either, and she ended up finishing the flight on her own. But she made it - and she had fun doing it. "Happy to not have given up," she said, adding that there's still plenty to work on, but that it'll come. She also had a word of thanks for Guillaume, whose work on her glider's centre of gravity meant she could finally keep pace with the others in the climbs.

## [Wannes](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1192993)

Wannes had no idea what was coming when he lined up at the start. A wave system had formed, and before the start he climbed up into it - then had to dive back down below the start altitude limit once he crossed the line. He pulled back into the wave shortly after and rode it for around 50 kilometres of the task, though it later turned out he'd been flying it a touch too slowly, a mistake he only discovered once the time was already lost.

From there, Wannes found the French team and flew the rest of the task in their gaggle. As the cumulus clouds grew more sporadic, strong thermals kept the pace high. Back near the wave system, he tried climbing back in for an easy final glide - a few pilots managed it, but he wasn't among them. One more thermal later, though, he had his final glide, with barely any time lost. "One of the most fun flights I've had in competition," he said afterwards.

Three very different days in the air, but all pointing the same way: forward — Wannes calling it one of his most fun flights yet, Michiel climbing the rankings again, and Edwige finding her footing, and her fun, after a rough start. Onwards to the next task.
