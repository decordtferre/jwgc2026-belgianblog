If there was one thing the whole Belgian team could agree on today, it's that nobody really knew what the sky had in store. Forecasts offered more questions than answers. Here's how the day unfolded, pilot by pilot. 

*PS: you can click on the pilots their names to watch their flights on WeGlide ;)!*

===

## [Michiel](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1189177)

With rain showers threatening the first sector, the plan was to start early, tag the second sector, and then keep flying for as long as it took to fill the two-hour task time. Michiel set off behind the German pilots, but when they turned back only minutes later, he chose to press on alone. It didn't take long before he found himself low - saved just in time by a solid thermal. From there, the energy lines improved on the way toward Aalen, though a wide blue gap still had to be crossed before reaching the edge of the second turnpoint. Once he found a strong climb there, it was simply a matter of bringing it home. A good day out overall - and for once, Skysight got the forecast right.

## [Wannes](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1189361)

Nobody could quite predict what the weather would do before takeoff. Once airborne, only a handful of cumulus had formed, some 10 kilometres ahead of the start line, with a few more building in the distance toward the first turnpoint. By the time Wannes started, though, those clouds were still far off, meaning a long glide to reach them. They turned out to be strong once he got there, and he picked up good speed on the way to the second point - though another long glide was needed to reach the single row of cumulus further on. Those were only so-so, and flying a poor line underneath them cost him valuable time. The final glide home was long once more, but this time with far more margin to spare. Looking back, Wannes knows he could have flown it better - starting too early was one of a few mistakes - but he still had a great time out there.

## [Edwige](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1189436)

Edwige left on her final PEV — the button pilots press to open their own personal start window - just as she saw the Czech and Swiss pilots heading out too. It turned out to be a fake start, though, leaving her alone in the blue for a tough opening leg. The air felt completely calm until she found a thermal in the first area, just enough to reach the first cumulus popping up on the way to the second. Good climbs followed, though she later found herself boxed in by a large Club Class gaggle — a shame, since some company would have been welcome on that lonely first leg, even if, in strong conditions, climbing with such a big group rarely pays off anyway. She landed happy: she feels more at home in the Discus with every flight, even if it's still frustrating not to match the pace of the other teams just yet. Tomorrow is another day.

## [Virginie](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1189509)

After an unfortunate outlanding the day before, Virginie bounced back in style: today she had the honour of flying the sniffer launch in the Aalen club's Duo Discus FES, testing the air before the rest of the field took to the sky.

Four very different flights, but one common thread ran through all of them: a sky that refused to give straight answers. The weather for tomorrow isn't that promising, but we're hoping for a nice third flying day! Results and photos below!
