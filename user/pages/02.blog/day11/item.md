Today was a fast one. The forecast promised good conditions, and the task area delivered - pilots were covering ground quicker than almost any day so far this championship. But speed came with a catch: rain was closing in on the task area, and getting home before it arrived became just as important as flying fast. In the end, Michiel ended up in the 3rd place on today's leaderbord!

===

## [Michiel](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1201309)

Michiel had arguably his quickest flight of the competition, sticking closely to his plan for most of the task but pushing the pace a touch harder than intended along the way - finishing around five minutes ahead of schedule. With hindsight, an extra 5km in the first sector might have squeezed out even more, but that's a small price to pay for the fastest speed he's ever flown. He also timed his return perfectly, landing just ahead of the rain and completing about half of the task before conditions closed in. The result speaks for itself: third place on the daily score, and a jump into the top ten of the overall clubclass leaderboard.

## [Edwige](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1201487)

Edwige broke her own speed record again today. She started well alongside the French team, sitting slightly lower before having to peel off and climb on her own after the first turnpoint. From there, the conditions turned bombastic - she barely needed to turn again as she pushed through, optimising her line through the second area before turning back just ahead of the advancing rain. The way home wasn't easy: rain returned along with a headwind, eating steadily into the buffer she'd built up over the last kilometres before the finish ring. She made it back to Aalen safely and is taking the evening to recover.

## [Wannes](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1201493)

Wannes had a solid flight overall, with one low point along the way. On the final glide, he needed a ratio of just 1:17 to make it home over the last 30km - a target that should normally be very achievable. But conditions worked against him: with the rain moving in, his actual glide ratio dropped to just 12.5, well short of what he needed, and he ended up landing out. It was a frustrating way to end an otherwise good flight, but he wasn't alone: plenty of pilots landed out today, and even several who did make it home picked up penalty points for finishing too low.

The coming days are expected to bring trickier weather than what the team has flown in so far, which is a good reminder of what this championship really is: a marathon, not a sprint. Every good flight counts, but so does staying safe and consistent when the sky doesn't cooperate. Right now, the Belgian juniors are keeping in their mind that sometimes winning is simply about not losing - and that mindset is exactly what will carry the team through the harder days ahead.
