After a long briefing, the first training tasks were set: a 2h AAT in club class, and a 200km speed task in standard class.

Wannes decided not to fly, but he's starting to feel better and should be in full shape once the competition starts. Michiel and Virginie took off for the AAT. Conditions were expected to be blue at the start, with a bit of Cu over the Alb again, but a rain zone was moving in for the evening. Michiel didn't want to fly too much due to the heat, and started together with a pilot from the Dutch juniors. They expected blue conditions, but the first wisps started forming just after crossing the start line. Virginie started a bit later. The thermals were even stronger than the day before, but the energy lines were harder to find, as the convergence wasn't marked by the same bombastic Cu's as yesterday. Flying club class gliders above 100 km/h isn't something you get to do every day — we flew extremely fast, but so did the competition. Interesting days ahead.

In standard class, Edwige wanted to explore more of the eastern region. Since the task setting wasn't optimal for the given conditions, she opted for a recon flight instead. This let her explore the energy lines available along the Nördlinger Ries, a crater formed by a meteor impact 15 million years ago, about 24km in diameter — a stunning piece of landscape. Given the heat, she also treated herself to ice cream and a cooldown in the swimming pool.

Team Belgium ended the day in paradise, enjoying the traditional tapping of a barrel of local beer (taste test completed, of course). Tomorrow looks like a smaller window. Wannes, Michiel, and Virginie have scrutineering scheduled for the day, with some smaller flights likely afterward.
