With two blue days in a row, the priority was simple: don't lose ground. Conditions again offered no clouds to read, forcing every gaggle to feel its way across the task on instinct and teamwork alone.

===

## [Michiel](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1205666)

With two blue days ahead, the main goal was staying safe on points rather than chasing a big result. Michiel started together with Wannes at around 14:50, expecting roughly three hours on task while their direct competitors kept playing the waiting game before the start line. After the first transition he found himself low, but managed to work his way out of it, and from there joined the gaggle heading towards Klippeneck, crossing paths with the Grand Prix class along the way. At Klippeneck the inversion sat much higher than expected, and with sharp coaching both on the ground and from the team back in Belgium, it became clear the glide after the final turnpoint would be a long one, with everyone struggling to the south. The gaggle chose to push north instead, a call that paid off in the end. With that, tomorrow's final day suddenly looks very interesting.

## [Edwige](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1205967)

Another tricky day unfolded in the blue for Edwige. She left early alongside the Czech and British pilots, though that start turned out not to be the strongest strategic choice. Low points came a few times on the first leg, but the gaggle caught up with her and she managed to hang on and make it home with them. A strong climb before the third turnpoint (thanks, FX) saved the day and let her overtake the very group she'd started with. With the last day of the competition ahead and blue skies expected again, the mood is all about pushing forward.

## [Wannes](https://www.weglide.org/flight/1205842)

The morning looked much like the day before, so the plan was to make it around the task in a small group. Wannes started with just two others but ended up flying with nearly the whole field until after Klippeneck. The first leg stayed modest, topping out around 2200m MSL (1300m AGL) with thermals in the 2-3 m/s range, before the ceiling jumped to 2900m MSL near Klippeneck in a stronger 4.5-5 m/s climb. What followed was a long glide with nothing underneath it, though a timely warning from Michiel let him conserve energy through the gap. A low save eventually turned up a thermal, and the final leg was flown mostly between 500 and 800m AGL until the final-glide climb finally appeared. Getting home safely was the main relief of the day.

A day of long, clawing glides and hard-won saves for all three pilots, but with everyone safely home and the final day looming, the championship is set up for one last push.
